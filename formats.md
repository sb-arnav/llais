# Format register library

Voice is constant — your stance, your eye for detail, your ear for rhythm. **Register** is what changes between a cold email and a short story. This is the deep version of Layer 3 in SKILL.md: for each format, what to lean on, what to cut, and how to open and close.

The rule under all of them: write to one specific reader, take a position, anchor abstractions in real detail, vary the rhythm. The format only tunes the dials.

---

## Cold email / outreach

**Reader state:** busy, skeptical, scanning. Assumes you want something.
**Length:** 60–110 words. One screen, no scroll.
**Lean on:** one concrete, specific reason you're writing *this* person (something they said, shipped, or wrote). A single clear, small ask. Honest numbers if you have them.
**Cut:** "I hope this email finds you well," "I wanted to reach out," "synergies," "circle back," fake urgency, your life story, three asks.
**Open:** with them, not you. Reference the specific thing.
**Close:** one low-friction ask with an out ("no worries if not").
**Tell to watch:** flattery that could be pasted into any email. If it isn't true of *only* this person, delete it.

## LinkedIn / X post

**Reader state:** scrolling, half-attention, allergic to performance.
**Length:** LinkedIn 80–150 words; X one thought, ruthless.
**Lean on:** one real opinion or one small true story. A first line that earns the second.
**Cut:** the "punchy fragment → aphorism" cadence (`Short line.\n\nAnother.\n\nProfound close.`) — it now reads as pure AI. The humble-brag framed as a lesson. Tricolon listicles ("3 things that changed my life").
**Open:** with a concrete moment or a claim you'll defend, not a thesis statement.
**Close:** plainly, or with a genuine question — not a manufactured mic-drop.
**Tell to watch:** if every line is the same length, it's a drone. Break it.

## Essay / blog post

**Reader state:** gave you their attention on purpose; will leave the moment it feels generic.
**Length:** as long as the idea earns, no longer.
**Lean on:** a thesis you actually believe, a visible path of thought, lived and specific detail, the curse-of-knowledge fix (show the joints — "what confused me was…").
**Cut:** the listicle skeleton, the per-section recap, the both-sides survey with no view, "In conclusion."
**Open:** in the middle of something concrete — a scene, a number, a claim. Not "X has become increasingly important."
**Close:** land the idea; don't summarize what you just said. Trust the reader to have read it.
**Tell to watch:** if a stranger couldn't state your position after reading, you wrote a survey, not an essay.

## Marketing / landing copy

**Reader state:** wary of being sold to; pattern-matches hype and tunes out.
**Length:** short. Every word fights for its place.
**Lean on:** the concrete outcome the reader feels (what's different on a Tuesday after they use it). Specific over superlative. Rhythm — copy is read in the ear.
**Cut:** "revolutionary," "cutting-edge," "seamless," "next-generation," "world-class," "game-changing." Adjective stacks. Claims with no image behind them.
**Open:** with the reader's problem or the outcome, in their words.
**Close:** one clear action.
**Tell to watch:** swap your brand name for a competitor's — if the copy still fits, it says nothing.

## Technical writing / docs / dev posts

**Reader state:** competent, time-pressed, wants the real tradeoff, smells condescension instantly.
**Length:** as short as completeness allows.
**Lean on:** exact terms, real examples, the honest caveat ("this breaks when…"), the actual tradeoff stated plainly.
**Cut:** "Let's dive in," "Let's break this down," "powerful," "simply," "just" (it's never just), over-hedging, dumbing-down.
**Open:** with what the thing is and the tradeoff it makes.
**Close:** the edge cases and when *not* to use it.
**Tell to watch:** "simply" and "just" usually sit in front of the part that isn't simple.

## Fiction / narrative

**Reader state:** wants to be somewhere, with someone; bored by being told how to feel.
**Lean on:** sensory and specific detail, subtext (what's not said), distinct character diction, scene over summary.
**Cut:** purple prose, adverb-laden dialogue tags ("she said angrily"), telling the emotion instead of showing the cause, the tidy moral at the end.
**Open:** in a concrete moment with a person doing something.
**Close:** on an image or an action, not an explanation.
**Tell to watch:** if you named the emotion, you probably skipped the thing that would have made the reader feel it.

## Slack / chat / text / replies

**Reader state:** mid-conversation, wants the point.
**Length:** as short as is kind and clear.
**Lean on:** contractions, fragments, getting to it, normal human warmth.
**Cut:** headers, bullet lists for two items, sign-offs, "I hope this helps," formal transitions, restating the question before answering it.
**Open:** with the answer.
**Close:** when you're done. No wrap-up.
**Tell to watch:** if a chat message has a bolded heading, you're writing a memo at a person.

## Calibrating fast

Ask three questions before the first sentence:
1. **Who exactly reads this, and what are they doing while they read?** (Sets length and register.)
2. **What's the one thing they should leave with?** (Sets the spine — cut everything off it.)
3. **What would I actually say if this person were across the table?** (Sets the voice — write that, then tidy only what speech can't carry.)
