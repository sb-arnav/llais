# Why Your First Engineering Hire Matters

The first engineer I hired had a worse résumé than three people I passed on. He'd also rewritten his side project's backend twice because he kept finding it embarrassing. I almost missed that detail. It turned out to be the only one that mattered.

Most founders treat hire number one like a senior slot to fill. They screen for the cleanest GitHub and the most familiar stack, then act surprised when the person freezes the first time the assignment is "go talk to five users and figure out what we should build."

Your first engineer sets what normal looks like for everyone after them. How fast you ship. Whether ugly-but-working code is allowed to exist while you learn. Whether someone will say "this is the wrong thing to build" in a meeting instead of nodding and writing it anyway.

So I stopped hiring for pedigree. I started hiring for the person who'd already, somewhere, done the unglamorous thing nobody asked them to do — and who could talk about being wrong without flinching. The spotless candidates almost never could.
